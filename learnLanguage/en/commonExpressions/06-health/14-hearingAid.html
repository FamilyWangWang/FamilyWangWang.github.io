第14章：助听器与听力检查（Hearing Aid / Audiologist）

场景背景

50岁左右，很多人开始注意到听力下降，特别是在嘈杂环境中听不清别人说话、需要把电视音量调大、或者经常要求别人重复。这是年龄相关听力损失（age-related hearing loss / presbycusis）的常见表现。听力学家（audiologist）可以进行听力测试（hearing test / audiogram）并推荐助听器（hearing aids）。现代助听器非常小巧且功能强大，但价格不菲。

关键文化差异：

  • 🇺🇸 美国：助听器非常贵（$1000-$6000/对），Medicare不覆盖助听器（但2022年的FDA新规允许销售非处方助听器OTC hearing aids，价格更低）
  • 🇬🇧 英国：NHS提供免费助听器，但选择有限；私人购买约£500-£3500/对
  • 美国人戴助听器的耻辱感（stigma）在减少，越来越多名人公开使用
  • 听力损失如果不处理，可能导致社交孤立和认知下降

完整对话

子场景1：意识到听力问题（Recognizing Hearing Loss）

Wife: Wei, did you hear what I just said? I've been calling your name from the kitchen.

正式: Wei, did you hear what I just said? I have been calling your name from the kitchen. 中文: Wei，你听到我刚说的了吗？我在厨房叫你呢。 详解: 家人是最先注意到你听力变化的人。✅推荐

You: Sorry, what? I didn't catch that. The TV was up kind of loud.

正式: Sorry, what did you say? I did not hear you. The television volume was elevated. 中文: 抱歉，什么？我没听清。电视声音有点大。 详解: "Didn't catch that" = 没听清/没听到（口语化的"没听清"）。"Kind of loud" = 有点大声。把责任推给电视音量是很多人的第一反应。✅推荐

Wife: The TV is always "kind of loud" these days. Honestly, I think you should get your hearing checked. You've been saying "what?" a lot more lately. And at dinner last week, you completely missed what David was saying.

正式: The television is consistently loud recently. I think you should have your hearing evaluated. You have been asking people to repeat themselves more frequently. At dinner last week, you did not hear what David was saying. 中文: 最近电视总是"有点大声"。说实话，我觉得你应该去检查一下听力。你最近经常说"什么？"。上周吃饭的时候，David说的你完全没听到。 详解: "Saying 'what?' a lot more" = 更经常说"什么？"。这是听力下降的典型症状——在嘈杂环境中更难听清。"Missed what sb was saying" = 没听到某人在说什么。家人的观察通常很准确。✅推荐

You: I'm not deaf. It's just... people mumble more these days.

正式: I am not deaf. People simply mumble more nowadays. 中文: 我不是聋了。只是... 现在人们说话更含糊了。 详解: "Mumble" = 含糊不清地说话。怪别人"说话含糊"是听力下降者的经典反应，在英语文化中也一样。"I'm not deaf" = 我不是聋了（防卫性回应）。✅推荐

Wife: (笑) That's literally what every person with hearing loss says. Look, it's no big deal. Dad got hearing aids last year and he says it changed his life. Just get it checked out — what have you got to lose?

正式: That is a common statement from people with hearing loss. It is not a significant issue. Dad obtained hearing aids last year and reports a dramatic improvement. Simply have it evaluated — what do you have to lose? 中文: （笑）那真的是每个听力下降的人都会说的话。听着，没什么大不了的。爸去年配了助听器，他说改变了他的生活。就去做个检查——你又不会损失什么？ 详解: "What have you got to lose?" = 你又不会损失什么？（鼓励某人行动的常用表达）。"No big deal" = 没什么大不了的。"Changed his life" = 改变了他的生活。"Get it checked out" = 去检查一下。✅推荐

子场景2：听力测试（Audiology Exam）

Audiologist: Hi Wei, I'm Dr. Taylor, the audiologist. I understand you've been having some trouble with your hearing. Before we do the test, tell me a little about what you've been experiencing.

正式: Hello Wei, I am Dr. Taylor, the audiologist. I understand you have been experiencing hearing difficulties. Before the test, please describe your symptoms. 中文: 你好Wei，我是Taylor医生，听力学家。我知道你在听力方面有些困难。在测试之前，跟我说说你经历了什么。 详解: "Audiologist" = 听力学家（不是医生，是听力学博士AuD）。"Trouble with your hearing" = 听力方面的困难。"Experiencing" = 经历的。✅推荐

You: Well, I have a hard time following conversations in noisy places — like restaurants or parties. I can hear people talking, I just can't make out the words. And I've been cranking up the TV volume more than I used to. My wife finally talked me into coming in.

正式: I have difficulty following conversations in noisy environments such as restaurants or parties. I can hear people speaking but cannot distinguish the words. I have been increasing the television volume more than before. My wife persuaded me to seek evaluation. 中文: 嗯，我在嘈杂的地方——比如餐厅或派对——很难跟上对话。我能听到别人说话，就是听不清说什么。而且我把电视音量调得比以前高。我妻子终于说服我来检查了。 详解: "Follow conversations" = 跟上对话/听懂对话。"Make out the words" = 听清/辨认出词语。"Cranking up" = 调高（音量）。这些是听力下降的典型描述。"Talked me into" = 说服我。✅推荐

Audiologist: What you're describing is very consistent with age-related hearing loss. It typically affects the higher frequencies first, which is why speech sounds muffled — you can hear the vowels but miss the consonants. Let's do an audiogram. I'm gonna have you put on these headphones and go into the sound booth. You'll hear a series of beeps at different pitches and volumes. Press this button whenever you hear a sound, even if it's very faint.

正式: Your description is consistent with age-related hearing loss, which typically affects higher frequencies first. This is why speech sounds muffled — vowels are audible but consonants are missed. Let us perform an audiogram. You will wear headphones in a sound booth and hear beeps at various pitches and volumes. Press this button whenever you detect a sound, even if faint. 中文: 你描述的情况和年龄相关听力损失非常一致。它通常先影响高频听力，所以语音听起来模糊——你能听到元音但错过了辅音。我们做一个听力图。我让你戴上这些耳机，进入隔音室。你会听到不同音调和音量的一系列嘟嘟声。当你听到声音时就按这个按钮，即使很微弱也要按。 详解: "Higher frequencies" = 高频。"Muffled" = 模糊的/闷的。"Audiogram" = 听力图。"Sound booth" = 隔音室。"Beeps" = 嘟嘟声。"Pitches" = 音调。"Faint" = 微弱的。"Make out" = 辨认出。这些是听力测试的标准描述。✅推荐

You: Okay, I think I'm ready. How long does this take?

正式: I am ready. How long does the test take? 中文: 好的，我想我准备好了。这要多久？ 详解: 标准的听力测试大约20-30分钟。✅推荐

Audiologist: About 20 to 25 minutes. Just relax and focus on the sounds. Afterwards, we'll go over the results together and I'll explain exactly what's going on.

正式: Approximately 20 to 25 minutes. Please relax and focus on the sounds. Afterwards, we will review the results together and I will explain the findings. 中文: 大约20到25分钟。放松，集中注意力听声音。之后我们一起看结果，我会详细解释情况。 详解: "Go over the results" = 一起看/过一遍结果。"What's going on" = 怎么回事/什么情况。✅推荐

子场景3：讨论结果和助听器（Results & Hearing Aids）

Audiologist: Alright Wei, here's your audiogram. I'll walk you through it. This side shows the different pitches, from low to high, and this side shows the volume. The further down the marks are, the more hearing loss you have. As you can see, you've got mild to moderate hearing loss in the higher frequencies on both ears. That's textbook presbycusis — age-related hearing loss.

正式: Here is your audiogram. This axis shows pitches from low to high, and this shows volume. Lower marks indicate greater hearing loss. You have mild to moderate high-frequency hearing loss in both ears. This is typical presbycusis — age-related hearing loss. 中文: 好了Wei，这是你的听力图。我给你解释一下。这边是不同的音调，从低到高，这边是音量。标记越往下，听力损失越严重。如你所见，你双耳高频都有轻到中度的听力损失。这就是典型的老年性耳聋——年龄相关听力损失。 详解: "Walk you through it" = 带你过一遍/详细解释。"Textbook" = 教科书式的/典型的。"Mild to moderate" = 轻到中度。"Presbycusis" = 老年性耳聋。"Both ears" = 双耳。✅推荐

You: So I do need hearing aids? It's not just in my head?

正式: Do I need hearing aids? It is not psychosomatic? 中文: 那我确实需要助听器？不是我的心理作用？ 详解: "Not just in my head" = 不是我的心理作用/不是我想象的。很多人担心听力问题被别人觉得是"装"的，确认诊断反而让他们安心。✅推荐

Audiologist: It's definitely not in your head. Hearing aids would make a real difference for you. Now, there are several types — behind-the-ear, in-the-ear, and the newer receiver-in-canal style, which is what most of my patients go with. They're small, discreet, and most people won't even notice you're wearing them.

正式: It is not psychosomatic. Hearing aids would benefit you significantly. There are several types — behind-the-ear, in-the-ear, and receiver-in-canal, which most patients prefer. They are small, discreet, and largely unnoticeable. 中文: 绝对不是你的心理作用。助听器对你会有真正的帮助。现在有几种类型——耳背式、耳内式、和较新的受话器入耳式，这也是我大多数病人选的。它们小巧、隐蔽，大多数人都不会注意到你戴着。 详解: "Behind-the-ear (BTE)" = 耳背式。"In-the-ear (ITE)" = 耳内式。"Receiver-in-canal (RIC)" = 受话器入耳式。"Discreet" = 隐蔽的/不显眼的。"Go with" = 选择。现代助听器确实非常小巧。✅推荐

You: What about those new over-the-counter hearing aids I keep seeing ads for? Are they any good?

正式: What about the over-the-counter hearing aids I see advertised? Are they effective? 中文: 那些我一直看到广告的非处方助听器呢？好用吗？ 详解: "OTC hearing aids" = 非处方助听器，2022年FDA批准，价格更便宜（$200-$1000）但只适合轻度到中度听力损失。"Keep seeing ads for" = 一直看到...的广告。"Any good" = 好用吗/有用吗。✅推荐

Audiologist: OTC hearing aids are fine for mild hearing loss, but you're in the mild-to-moderate range, bordering on moderate in some frequencies. I'd recommend prescription hearing aids — they're programmable to your specific hearing profile and have better noise cancellation. They do cost more, but they're more effective.

正式: Over-the-counter hearing aids are appropriate for mild hearing loss, but you have mild-to-moderate loss, bordering on moderate in certain frequencies. I recommend prescription hearing aids — they are programmable to your specific profile and offer superior noise cancellation. They cost more but are more effective. 中文: 非处方助听器对轻度听力损失可以，但你是轻到中度，某些频率接近中度了。我推荐处方助听器——它们可以根据你具体的听力状况编程，降噪也更好。确实更贵，但效果更好。 详解: "Bordering on" = 接近/几乎达到。"Programmable" = 可编程的。"Noise cancellation" = 降噪。"Your specific hearing profile" = 你具体的听力状况。✅推荐

You: How much are we looking at?

正式: What is the estimated cost? 中文: 大概多少钱？ 详解: "How much are we looking at?" = 大概多少钱？非常口语化的询价方式。✅推荐

Audiologist: A good pair of prescription hearing aids runs between $2,000 and $5,000 for both ears. That includes the fitting, programming, follow-up adjustments, and usually a three-year warranty. Some insurance plans cover part of it. I'd recommend checking with your provider.

正式: A pair of prescription hearing aids costs between $2,000 and $5,000 for both ears. This includes fitting, programming, follow-up adjustments, and typically a three-year warranty. Some insurance plans provide partial coverage. 中文: 一对好的处方助听器大约2000到5000美元（双耳）。包括适配、编程、后续调整和通常三年的保修。有些保险计划覆盖一部分。我建议你咨询你的保险公司。 详解: "Runs between... and..." = 价格在...到...之间。"Fitting" = 适配/调试。"Follow-up adjustments" = 后续调整。"Warranty" = 保修。助听器确实是笔不小的投资。"Provider" 这里指保险公司。✅推荐

You: That's a lot of money. Is it really worth it?

正式: That is a significant expense. Is it genuinely worthwhile? 中文: 这笔钱不少。真的值得吗？ 详解: "Worth it" = 值得的。问价值是完全合理的。✅推荐

Audiologist: I always tell my patients — hearing loss isn't just about missing sounds. It's about staying connected to the people you love. When you can't follow conversations, you start withdrawing, and that isolation can lead to depression and even cognitive decline. Studies show that treating hearing loss is one of the most impactful things you can do for your overall health as you age. Think of it as an investment in your quality of life.

正式: Hearing loss is not solely about missing sounds — it affects social connection. Difficulty following conversations leads to withdrawal, isolation, potential depression, and cognitive decline. Treating hearing loss significantly impacts overall health. Consider it an investment in your quality of life. 中文: 我总是告诉我的病人——听力损失不仅仅是错过声音的问题。它关乎和你爱的人保持联系。当你跟不上对话，你就开始退缩，这种孤立可能导致抑郁甚至认知下降。研究表明，处理听力损失是你随着年龄增长能为整体健康做的最有影响力的事情之一。把它看作对生活品质的投资。 详解: "Staying connected" = 保持联系/不脱离社交。"Withdrawing" = 退缩/退出社交。"Cognitive decline" = 认知下降。"Impactful" = 有影响力的。"Quality of life" = 生活品质。"Think of it as..." = 把它看作...。这是听力学家最有说服力的论点。✅推荐

You: When you put it that way... okay. Let's do it. When can I get fitted?

正式: Presented that way, I agree. When can I be fitted? 中文: 你这么一说的话... 好吧。做吧。什么时候可以配？ 详解: "When you put it that way" = 你这么说的话。"Let's do it" = 做吧。"Get fitted" = 配/适配。✅推荐

Audiologist: I can fit you as early as next week. I'll order a pair based on your audiogram, and at the fitting appointment, I'll program them specifically for your hearing needs and show you how to use and maintain them. Most people adjust within a couple of weeks. Your brain just needs a little time to get used to hearing sounds it's been missing.

正式: I can schedule a fitting as early as next week. I will order devices based on your audiogram, program them at the fitting appointment, and provide usage and maintenance instructions. Most patients adjust within a few weeks as the brain acclimates to restored sounds. 中文: 我最早下周就可以帮你适配。我会根据你的听力图订购一对，适配时我会按你的听力需求编程，教你如何使用和保养。大多数人两到三周就能适应。你的大脑只是需要一点时间来重新习惯那些它一直缺失的声音。 详解: "Get used to" = 习惯。"Missing" = 缺失的。"Adjust" = 适应。助听器确实需要适应期，一开始可能会觉得声音太吵，但大脑会逐渐适应。✅推荐

核心口语表达清单

必学表达 ✅推荐

  1. I didn't catch that. — 我没听清。
  2. I have a hard time in noisy places. — 我在嘈杂的地方有困难。
  3. I can't make out the words. — 我听不清词。
  4. Get your hearing checked. — 去检查一下听力。
  5. It's no big deal. — 没什么大不了的。
  6. What have you got to lose? — 你又不会损失什么？
  7. It's definitely not in your head. — 绝对不是你的心理作用。
  8. How much are we looking at? — 大概多少钱？
  9. Is it really worth it? — 真的值得吗？
  10. Think of it as an investment in your quality of life. — 把它看作对生活品质的投资。

了解即可 👂了解

  1. I've got tinnitus — constant ringing. — 我有耳鸣——一直响。
  2. My hearing comes and goes. — 我的听力时好时坏。
  3. I need to read lips. — 我需要看口型。
  4. Can I connect it to my phone? — 能连手机吗？（现代助听器可以蓝牙连接手机）

避免说法 ❌避免

  1. ~~I am going deaf.~~ → I've been having trouble with my hearing.（"deaf" 太极端）
  2. ~~Only old people need hearing aids.~~ → Hearing loss can happen at any age.（错误观念）
  3. ~~Hearing aids are ugly.~~ → Modern hearing aids are very discreet.（刻板印象）
  4. ~~I don't want to look disabled.~~ → Wearing hearing aids shows you're proactive about your health.（心态转变）

注意事项

🇺🇸 美国助听器须知

  • OTC助听器：2022年FDA批准，轻度听力损失可选OTC（$200-$1000）
  • 处方助听器：中重度需要，$2000-$6000/对
  • Medicare不覆盖：Original Medicare不覆盖助听器（但有些Medicare Advantage计划可能覆盖部分）
  • FSA/HSA：可以用灵活支出账户支付
  • 试戴期：大多数听力学家提供30-60天的试用期
  • Costco：Costco的助听器中心价格通常比私人诊所低很多

🇬🇧 英国助听器须知

  • NHS免费：NHS提供免费助听器，但型号选择有限
  • 等待时间：NHS可能需要等几周
  • 私人助听器：选择更多，约£500-£3500/对
  • Battery或充电式：两种都有，充电式更方便但更贵
  • NHS后续服务：免费维护和调整

💡 实用建议

  • 不要因为"面子"问题拒绝助听器——现代助听器几乎看不见
  • 听力损失与认知下降有明确关联，早干预很重要
  • 适应助听器需要2-4周，一开始觉得声音奇怪是正常的
  • 助听器不能恢复正常听力，但能显著改善生活质量
  • 定期清洁和维护助听器，延长使用寿命
  • 如果买处方助听器，比较不同听力学家（audiologist）的价格和服务
  • Costco的助听器性价比很高，值得考虑
